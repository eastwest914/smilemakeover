FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between porcelain veneers and composite veneers?

Porcelain veneers are custom-made in a dental laboratory from high-strength ceramic and generally offer greater longevity, translucency, and stain resistance. Composite veneers are sculpted directly onto the tooth using tooth-colored resin, which is typically a faster, lower-cost option that can often be completed in one visit.

How long do dental veneers usually last?

Longevity varies by material, oral hygiene, and habits such as grinding or biting hard objects. Porcelain veneers commonly last 10 to 15 years with good care, while composite veneers often need repair or replacement sooner. Your dentist can give an estimate for your situation.

Is getting porcelain veneers painful?

Most patients tolerate the process well. Local anesthesia is used when needed during tooth preparation, and temporary veneers are placed while your custom restorations are made. Some sensitivity afterward is possible and usually temporary.

How do I maintain my veneers after placement?

Care for them like natural teeth: brush twice daily with non-abrasive fluoride toothpaste, floss regularly, and keep up with routine cleanings and checkups. If you grind your teeth, ask about a night guard.

What can I expect during a veneer consultation?

Your dentist will evaluate your teeth and gums, discuss your goals, take digital images or impressions, and outline a treatment plan, including material options, timeline, and cost. Call 561-485-1193 to schedule yours.

Results and longevity vary by patient. Veneer candidacy is determined after an in-person exam.
